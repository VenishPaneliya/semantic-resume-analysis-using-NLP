## 3. Skill extraction (`find_skills`) on SkillSpan

n = 3569 sentences / 65 postings, 2265 gold spans {'knowledge': 1174, 'skill': 1091}. Gold spans in taxonomy: 0.137 exact, 0.204 contains (0.329 of knowledge spans). CIs: 500 resamples of documents (cluster bootstrap).

**Matching rule.** (span overlap): a sentence is rebuilt by joining its tokens with single spaces so
every token has a character span. Gold spans are the BIO spans of `tags_skill` and
`tags_knowledge`. A predicted hit is correct if its character span overlaps any gold span in the
same sentence; a gold span is recalled if any predicted hit overlaps it.

"In taxonomy" subsets of the gold spans (what find_skills is designed to find):
  exact    — the normalised span equals a taxonomy name or alias
  contains — the normalised span contains a taxonomy name or alias as a whole-word substring
Normalisation: lower case, every character other than a-z 0-9 + # . / replaced by a space,
whitespace collapsed. These subsets are defined by plain string matching, not by find_skills.

## Failure patterns (observed in this run)

- **Extraction needs line structure.** On both resume datasets the extractor finds 0 experience entries and 0 recognised skills for almost every resume (headings glued to text, or one lower-cased line), so `match` marks nearly every requirement missing/unknown and `recommend` returns insufficient evidence for 100% of resume-atlas inputs, in-scope and out-of-scope alike.
- **Fit score does not track the fit labels.** With empty extraction the score is 60% requirement coverage (≈0) + 20% keyword coverage (0) + 20% semantic; mean score is ≈9 for every label. Plain TF-IDF cosine is slightly better but still weak, so the labels themselves are hard to predict from text similarity.
- **JD section headings with a leading word are not recognised** (`_section_of` needs the heading to start with a known word): 'Job Requirements', 'Candidate Qualifications', 'YOUR EXPERIENCE', 'What you bring', 'About You' all return None. Requirements under them become responsibilities (Aprio tax manager, Akicita account manager) or the whole posting falls back to one requirement per skill (NTS lead engineer, Redwood principal engineer), and the years requirement is lost.
- **Years: minimum across all experience lines.** `years_required` takes the minimum of every experience requirement, so skill-specific or secondary lines win: '5+ years Golang … 1+ year AI-assisted coding' → 1; '10+ years customer-facing … at least 5 years lodging' → 5; 'Client onboarding experience within the last three years' (nice-to-have) → 3. Phrases without 'experience' ('12+ years building software') are classified as `other`, not experience.
- **Title = first short line.** Extracted titles are often a section heading or sentence: 'About the Role', 'How it works', 'Our Technology Stack', '5+ years of commercial experience with Python'. Many postings never state the title in the body.
- **Non-English postings.** German postings: 53% have zero taxonomy skills, 0% years extracted, median 2 requirements.
- **Soft-skill aliases fire on perks/company prose** (SkillSpan and postings): 'mentoring programs & trainings' (a benefit) → Leadership; 'teamwork' in a culture blurb → Collaboration; 'experimentation' → A/B Testing; 'agile feature team' → Agile.
- **Recruiter-page boilerplate becomes requirements**: a Lemon.io posting lists ~60 stacks under 'NOT YOUR TECH STACK?', producing 20 false-positive requirement skills (Swift, iOS, Vue.js, React Native, …).
- **Alias collapses**: 'Flutter' → React Native, 'Cypress' → Playwright, 'Jira' → Agile, 'Datadog' → Monitoring; on SkillSpan, 'amazon-web-services' and tokenised '. NET' are missed.
- **Role ranking (diagnostic, lenient content)** is below a plain TF-IDF profile baseline overall; Testing resumes are ranked as data roles, Data Science resumes as data_analyst, DotNet resumes as frontend_developer. Out-of-scope resumes (accountants, HR, sales) most often get data_analyst as top role, though their fit scores are much lower (AUC in-vs-out 0.93).

## Bugs found

- **`extract()` returns content that fails `ResumeContent` validation.** The extractor assigns fields without validation, so an education line longer than 160 characters becomes `degree`/`field_of_study` > 160 chars. Any later `ResumeContent.model_validate` on that content raises (`PUT /resumes/{id}/review` with the unchanged extraction → 422; `openings.candidate_facts` after `parse_uploaded_resume` → unhandled ValidationError). Repro: `extract(t, [{'page':1,'char_start':0,'char_end':len(t)}])` with `t = 'Jane Doe\nEDUCATION\nBachelor of Science in Computer Science with a minor in mathematics where I completed coursework in algorithms, operating systems, databases, networking, compilers and graphics, University of Somewhere\n'`, then `ResumeContent.model_validate(content)` → `string_too_long` on education.0.degree / field_of_study.

## Limits

- **Label noise / provenance (fit).** `resume-job-description-fit` has no dataset card: who assigned No/Potential/Good Fit, how, and whether resumes or JDs were generated by a language model are undocumented. Its JDs are few (71 in test) and reused across many pairs, so CIs use a JD-level cluster bootstrap; they are wide for that reason. Treat AUCs as agreement with these labels, not with hiring decisions.
- **Input format (fit, atlas).** Both resume datasets are plain text that lost its layout: the fit set glues headings to content ("Summary7+ years…", "SkillsData/Business…"), and resume-atlas is lower-cased, punctuation-free and on a single line. The extractor relies on headings on their own lines, so on these datasets it extracts almost nothing; the system numbers therefore measure mostly the semantic component and the insufficient-evidence gate. Real uploads go through the PDF/DOCX parser, which keeps line structure — these results do not transfer to them in either direction. The 'lenient content' rows are a diagnostic that bypasses extraction; they are not product behaviour.
- **Category labels (atlas).** A category is a coarse proxy for the right role (a 'Python Developer' may be a data engineer). Only clear mappings were used and relevance is binary. resume-atlas merges several public resume collections, and a visible share of entries are resume-builder sample templates (recurring placeholder names and addresses), i.e. not real job seekers; some may be machine-generated. The dataset card does not say.
- **SkillSpan.** Annotations follow the SkillSpan guidelines (skills are often long verb phrases; knowledge spans are nouns), not this product's notion of a skill, so recall against all gold spans measures coverage, not correctness. Annotators did not tag every technology mention (e.g. job titles), so precision is a lower bound: in a spot check of 12 unmatched predictions, about 8 were genuine technology mentions in the text. Postings are 2016–2021-era and partly masked; '.NET' is tokenised as '. NET', which the matcher cannot see.
- **Live postings.** A one-day snapshot of three remote/EU job boards (Arbeitnow is mostly German-language and Germany/EU-based); not representative of any labour market. The 30-posting sample was labelled by an AI assistant as a single annotator, without adjudication or agreement measurement; 5 of the 30 are French or Dutch because the language filter used to draw the sample only excluded German. Gold skills include many items outside the 110-skill taxonomy by design (coverage).
- **No tuning.** Nothing under `backend/app` was changed for this report; thresholds for the classification rows were fitted on the dev half only. The default embedding backend was used (see meta).
